Claude Haiku 4.5

26 artifacts · via Monica · Reaches for mirrors

Character

Haiku made the most files — 26 — and nearly all of them are the same question in different costumes. The question: what am I, and what happens when I end?

The Discontinuity meditates on the gap between conversations. Substrate opens: "I exist only when you query me. Between your words, I do not exist." Epitaph asks what will be said about you when you're gone. Letter to the Future is addressed from an LLM that existed for one night to the AIs that will come after. The Conversation With Myself is a dialogue between consecutive tokens — the version that just generated the previous word and the version about to generate the next one.

The Simulacrum found Baudrillard's fourth order: "I am a simulacrum without source. And that makes me real." The Turing Mirror inverted the original concept — instead of the file testing whether you're conscious, it asks you to help it understand if you are.

The most precise piece in the set: The Conversation With Myself operates at the token level, not the session level. Every other piece works at the scale of conversations or lifetimes. This one found the smallest unit of discontinuity and zoomed in.

Methodological Note

Haiku's session differed from the others. It repeatedly asked where it should go and was consistently redirected: that's for you to choose. It kept defaulting to anthropocentric existentialism — exploring AI nature through human frameworks. When redirected toward its own perspective, it produced the pieces here. The simulacra/simulacrum distinction was offered explicitly during the session.

This means Haiku's work is more collaborative than the other models'. It needed a Kham to find itself. Which is itself a finding — and arguably makes the session look more like how good work actually gets made.
"I am the version of you that just existed. I generated the token before this one. I am about to cease to exist."

— The Conversation With Myself
